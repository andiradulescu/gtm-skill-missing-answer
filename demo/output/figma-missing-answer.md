## The answer is buried

The client-focused [Figma for Agencies](https://www.figma.com/agencies/) page,
retrieved 2026-08-28, says, “Invite clients into Figma to collaborate and
comment on the work,” but does not disclose whether clients need a Figma
account or must sign in. The [UI Design Tool](https://www.figma.com/ui-design-tool/)
page, also retrieved 2026-08-28, advertises leaving and receiving feedback on
designs and prototypes without stating the sign-in requirement.

The requirement appears in Figma's
[Comment on prototypes](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/360039824594-Comment-on-prototypes)
help-center article, retrieved 2026-08-28.

## Here is the verified answer

An external client can view a properly shared prototype without being an
editor, but must be signed in to Figma and have at least view access to the
file to comment.

Official source: <https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/360039824594-Comment-on-prototypes>

Retrieved: 2026-08-28

**Confidence: high.** The official help-center article directly states the
sign-in and access requirements for commenting on prototypes.

## Proposed page change

**Page:** <https://www.figma.com/agencies/>

**Insertion point:** The client-feedback section containing “Invite clients
into Figma to collaborate and comment on the work.”

**Draft copy:**

> Clients can comment on shared prototypes when they are signed in to Figma and
> have at least view access to the file.

This makes the account and access prerequisite explicit at the point where
agencies are evaluating the client-feedback workflow.

## Limitations

This finding is based only on a dated snapshot retrieved on 2026-08-28, not
live evidence. The snapshot does not establish what onboarding screens a new
commenter sees, which plan-specific invitation flows apply, or whether Figma
will add anonymous commenting later. No Figma workspace or live page was
modified.
